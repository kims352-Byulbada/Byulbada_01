01 Start with what hasn’t changed

The first question was meant to measure distance — student then, teacher now. He answered it backwards.

“Start with, how is it the same? It’s still about communication.”

“We might use motion more than still or static print. But the basic goals are still the same.”

“Obviously there were a lot of typefaces then, but they weren’t necessarily democratized as they are now.”

02 Good, pretty, or neither

He wouldn’t define the difference. He gave four things instead, and let them mark out the range.

“If it’s a train schedule or a plane schedule, you want it to be functional and good and easy to follow. You’re not necessarily looking for it to be pretty.”

“Massimo Vignelli’s subway poster is good. But some people can consider it also pretty.”

“The film poster for La La Land — that’s just a pretty image. Is it good? Maybe, maybe not.”

“If I go to JFK and look at the signage and I can find my way around easily, then it’s doing its job. It’s not pretty, but it’s good.”

03 The question he asks instead

Asked what he notices in student work, he declined the frame outright — and replaced it with two questions he actually uses.

“I don’t necessarily want to think of it as good or bad. It’s more about — how can it be improved?”

“What is the goal, and how are form and content coming together?”

“When form and content doesn’t go together, there’s a disconnect.”

“Good and bad is about taste. That’s part of the equation, but it’s not really the only thing that’s going to solve it.”

04 Taste moves, 
and that’s the point

Then he undercut his own standard, and explained why the class begins with an assignment about what you happen to like. He is right about me. Three years ago, at my previous college in Korea, I couldn’t stand Helvetica — I thought there was nothing in it to see.

“I don’t necessarily like the design of Disney things. Moana — it’s bad type. But it’s bad in terms of taste for me, and for people who like Disney, it’s good.”

“Two years from now you’re going to completely change your mind about what you think is good design.”

“Part of it is just creating a benchmark for yourself — sophomore year, this is what I liked.”
